## Llapingachos

- **Source:** Layla Pujol, “Llapingachos: Ecuadorian stuffed potato patties,” Laylita’s Recipes — https://www.laylita.com/recipes/llapingachos-or-stuffed-potato-patties/
- **Why this source:** Pujol presents the recipe in the first person, describes growing up with Ecuadorian food elsewhere on the site, and supplies a complete ingredient list, method, practical troubleshooting, timing, and yield.
- **Exact evidence:** 5 large Russets (about 3 lb), 2 tbsp sunflower or avocado oil, 1/2 cup white onion, 2 tsp ground achiote, 1 cup quesillo or mozzarella, salt to taste; boil until soft; cook the onion-achiote refrito about 5 minutes; mash and rest the dough about 1 hour; form roughly 12 golf-ball-size portions, fill with cheese, shape thick, refrigerate 30–60 minutes, and brown both sides on a well-heated dry griddle.
- **Ambiguities preserved:** The article says the dough should rest at least 1 hour while the card says about 1 hour; the shaped rest is described as at least 30 minutes in the article and 30–60 minutes in the card. Accompaniments have no quantities or in-page preparation and are presented in varying combinations. No potato-boiling water quantity or griddle temperature is stated.
- **Editorial descriptor:** Cheese-filled Ecuadorian potato patties with achiote refrito and a careful double rest.

## Papas Chorreadas

- **Source:** Erica Dinho, “Papas Chorreadas (Potatoes with Cream and Cheese Sauce),” My Colombian Recipes — https://www.mycolombianrecipes.com/potatoes-with-cheese-sauce-papas/
- **Why this source:** Dinho states that she was born and raised in Colombia and that the site draws on her Colombian family kitchen. The current English page is complete and identifies the dish with Cundinamarca.
- **Exact evidence:** 8 medium partially peeled red potatoes, 1 chicken-bouillon tablet and water; a sauce of 1 tbsp each butter and vegetable oil, 1 1/2 cups diced tomatoes, 3 scallions, garlic powder, cumin, salt and pepper, 1 cup heavy cream, 1 tbsp cilantro, and 1 cup shredded mozzarella. Cook potatoes about 30 minutes until tender and drain. In parallel, cook tomato and scallion 7 minutes, season and cook 2 minutes, then add cream, cilantro, and cheese and cook 3 minutes or until melted. Pour over potatoes and serve hot.
- **Ambiguities preserved:** The English card currently gives 1/4 tsp each garlic powder, cumin, and salt, whereas the Spanish card gives 1/2 tsp. Water and pepper are unquantified. The prose offers chicken broth instead of water plus bouillon, and the Spanish page also suggests vegetable broth for a vegetarian variation.
- **Editorial descriptor:** Cundinamarca red potatoes under a creamy, cheesy tomato-and-scallion hogao.

## From-Scratch Funeral Potatoes

- **Source:** Rosemary Mark, “From ‘Scratch’ Basic Funeral Potatoes,” Idaho Potato Commission — https://idahopotato.com/recipes/from-scratch-basic-funeral-potatoes
- **Why this source:** The Idaho Potato Commission hosts a full, clearly attributed recipe that makes the cream sauce from scratch and retains traditional casserole alternatives.
- **Exact evidence:** 2 lb thawed frozen Idaho hash browns/diced potatoes or 2 1/4 lb peeled, par-boiled, grated fresh Russets; 3 tbsp butter or olive oil; 1 cup onion; optional 3 oz mushrooms; 1/4 cup flour; 1 cup broth; 1 cup milk; salt and pepper; 2 cups sour cream or yogurt; 2 cups cheese; cornflake or panko topping plus 1/4 cup melted butter. Soften onion, optionally cook mushrooms, thicken the flour-broth-milk sauce, stir in cultured dairy and cheese, combine with potatoes, top, bake covered 45–60 minutes until bubbling, then uncovered 10–15 minutes until golden.
- **Ambiguities preserved:** The page prints “375°” without a scale; the YAML explicitly identifies 375 F as a context-supported inference from the US publisher and audience. Every OR choice remains unsimplified. The optional variation list permits meats, vegetables, or spinach but does not say whether combinations are intended. The assembled covered dish may be held refrigerated for up to 2 days.
- **Editorial descriptor:** A bubbling Idaho potato casserole with genuinely from-scratch cheese sauce and buttered crumbs.

## Potato Kugel

- **Source:** Shannon Sarna, “Potato Kugel,” Jewish Food Society — https://www.jewishfoodsociety.org/recipes/potato-kugel
- **Why this source:** Jewish Food Society supplies the complete family recipe, provenance, timing, yield, ingredients, and ten-step method; it is attributed to Sarna and her husband’s grandmother, Baba Billie.
- **Exact evidence:** 1/3 cup olive oil heated about 10 minutes in a 9-by-13-inch glass/Pyrex dish while the oven heats to 375 F; 8 peeled Yukon Golds and 2 onions shredded together; 5 eggs whisked and combined with 3/4 cup matzo meal, 1 1/2 tbsp fine sea salt, 2 tsp pepper, and 2 tsp garlic powder. Test the oil for a sizzle, spread the mixture evenly, garnish with paprika and coarse salt, and bake 40–50 minutes until crisp at the edges and golden on top. Cool slightly, cut, and serve warm or at room temperature.
- **Ambiguities preserved:** Regular or gluten-free matzo meal; sweet or hot paprika; unquantified garnish amounts; no duration for “cool slightly.”
- **Editorial descriptor:** Baba Billie’s crisp-edged, creamy-hearted holiday potato kugel.
