URL: https://www.limaeasy.com/peruvian-food-guide/typical-main-courses/papa-rellena

| Beef filling | 450 g minced beef; onion, garlic, tomato, tomato paste, 1–2 tbsp ají panca, 2–3 tbsp water, eggs, olives, raisins, parsley, seasonings; brown beef separately, simmer base, add parsley, cool, then add cold items | Hot vegetables and cold egg/olive additions are separate graph nodes; unquantified seasonings remain unquantified |

Editorial descriptor: Peruvian fried stuffed-potato dish with a chilled potato dough and cooled seasoned beef filling.

## Ocopa Arequipeña — Peru

Primary source: Cecilia Peterson, Smithsonian Folklife Festival, “Recipes from Peru: Chupe de Res and Ocopa Arequipeña”
URL: https://festival.si.edu/blog/2015/recipes-from-peru-chupe-de-res-ocopa-arequipena/

Official corroborating source reviewed: MIDAGRI / INIA, *Platos a base de papa*, Ocopa Arequipeña, pp. 17–18
Announcement: https://www.gob.pe/institucion/midagri/noticias/493673-midagri-lanza-recetario-de-platillos-infaltables-en-base-a-papa
PDF: https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/1898687/Recetario%20Papa%20pa%20ti.pdf.pdf

Why the Smithsonian recipe remains primary: it is a complete, explicitly identified family adaptation with a full potato-cooking step and a coherent ingredient-to-method mapping. The government collection confirms ocopa's place among Peruvian potato dishes but presents a materially different peanut, huacatay, bread, and cracker version and does not state how to cook its listed potatoes. Combining the two would create an invented hybrid.

| Evidence area | Source-backed facts | Model treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family adaptation | Walnuts replace the peanuts more usual in Peru; cream cheese and feta replace farmer's cheese | Recorded as adaptation provenance, not presented as a universal canonical formula |
| Potatoes | 6 medium-large waxy potatoes such as Yukon Gold; boil until tender; serve whole, halved, or sliced | No boiling duration is invented; all three serving shapes remain alternatives |
| Pepper and onion | Stem and seed 10 dried ají amarillo; dry-toast high until dark and fragrant; soak covered in hot water 15–20 minutes; brown/caramelize onion in 2 tbsp oil | Observable cues and the soak duration are preserved |
| Sauce | Blend peppers, onion, 10 walnuts, 3 oz cream cheese, 1 oz feta, 1/2 bunch cilantro, a few tbsp soaking liquid; slowly add 1/3 cup oil; salt | Only the stated few tablespoons of soaking liquid enter the sauce |
| Adjustment and serving | Crackers only if too spicy; more oil only for creamier texture; lettuce, eggs, olives, farmer's cheese | Corrections and garnishes remain optional or unquantified exactly where the source leaves them so |

Omissions and ambiguities:

- The potato-boiling water, pepper-soaking water, salt, corrective crackers or oil, and garnish quantities are absent.
- The source gives no potato boiling time or blending time.
- The MIDAGRI/INIA version is not used as a substitute because it is a distinct formula and has its own procedural omission.

Fidelity note: the graph retains the source family's walnut and mixed-cheese adaptation and does not silently replace it with the official collection's peanut-huacatay variant.

Editorial descriptor: Arequipa-style potato presentation with a creamy toasted-chile sauce, represented here by a documented Peruvian family adaptation.

## Carapulcra — Peru

Source: PROMPERÚ, Peru Travel, “Carapulcra”
URL: https://www.peru.travel/gastronomy/en/recipes/main-dishes/carapulcra.html

Omissions and ambiguities:

- The source gives no yield and no quantities for oil, broth, salt, pepper, rice, or yuca.
- Cumin appears once as 1/2 teaspoon and again in an unquantified seasoning phrase; no second amount is inferred.
- “A few minutes” remains source text for the short cooking phases.

Fidelity note: the pork is removed after browning and reintroduced with the dried potato, rather than being left in the pot during the seasoning-base phase.

Editorial descriptor: Peruvian dried-potato and pork stew finished with peanuts and port.
