## Tartiflette au Reblochon

- Editorial descriptor: A Savoyard skillet-to-oven potato bake built around a deliberately split whole Reblochon, with its rind-on top half forming the gratinated cap.
- Source: [Reblochon de Savoie AOP — Recette de la Tartiflette au Reblochon](https://www.reblochon.fr/nos-recettes/tartiflette-reblochon-de-savoie/)
- Provenance: First-party recipe from the Reblochon de Savoie AOP trade body; French-language source.
- Exact evidence: 1 Reblochon; 1 kg firm-fleshed potatoes; 200 g smoked bacon lardons; 2 large onions (about 200 g); 10 cl white wine; ground pepper; optional grated nutmeg; 4 people. Brown bacon and onion 3 minutes, add potatoes for 20 minutes over medium heat, deglaze 5 minutes, split the rind-on cheese horizontally, mix small pieces from one half into the potato mixture, place the other half rind-up on top, and bake 15–20 minutes at 200 C until golden. Serve hot with green salad and do not add salt.
- Ambiguities and limitations: Pepper and nutmeg are unquantified. The page says 25 minutes preparation and 20 minutes cooking, but the individual cooking steps total 43–48 minutes. The green salad is instructed for serving but absent from the ingredient list, so it is modeled as optional and unquantified.

## Jansson's Temptation (Janssons frestelse)

- Editorial descriptor: Sweden's holiday potato-and-sprat casserole, with onion softened without browning and breadcrumbs added only for the final 10–15 minutes.
- Source: [Sweden.se — Jansson’s temptation](https://sweden.se/culture/food/janssons-temptation)
- Provenance: Sweden.se is Sweden's official public information site, operated by the Swedish Institute; English-language source, last updated 13 March 2025.
- Exact evidence: 1.2 kg waxy potatoes; 2 large onions; 2 tins (125 g) spice-cured sprat fillets; 0.5 dl sprat brine; 5 dl double cream; 1 pinch salt; 2 pinches black pepper; 3 tbsp breadcrumbs; 1 knob butter; 6–8 servings. Preheat to 200 C, sauté thin onion in butter over low heat until soft but not brown, cut potatoes into thin strips, brush an ovenproof dish with butter, make a few potato/onion/sprat layers, pour over cream and brine while leaving 1–2 cm headroom, bake low in the oven 50–60 minutes, add breadcrumbs with 10–15 minutes left, finish until potatoes and crumbs are golden, and rest before serving.
- Ambiguities and limitations: The direct page returned HTTP 403 to the browsing client, but its full indexed page content was available and read. The ingredient line calls the one knob of butter "for frying," while the method also uses butter to brush the dish; the YAML exposes that unresolved split. Salt and pepper placement is not specified. Swedish ansjovis are spice-cured sprats, not Mediterranean anchovies.

## Kopytka

- Editorial descriptor: Polish little-hoof dumplings made from completely cooled smooth potato purée, a restrained amount of flour, and egg, then batch-boiled until soft.
- Source: [Kwestia Smaku — Kopytka](https://www.kwestiasmaku.com/przepis/kopytka)
- Provenance: Long-running Polish culinary publication with a complete Polish-language recipe and explicit quantities, method, tips, and serving suggestions.
- Exact evidence: 1 kg potatoes; 200 g flour; 1 egg; 1 tsp salt; 4 portions. Peel and cook potatoes in salted water, drain and immediately press perfectly smooth, cool completely, add flour, egg, and a pinch of salt, form a uniform dough, divide into six, roll thumb-thick on a floured board, flatten, cut 1.5–2 cm pieces, boil in batches in a large pot of salted water, stir gently, then cook about 3 minutes from floating or until soft and lift out with a slotted spoon.
- Ambiguities and limitations: The 200 g flour covers dough, board dusting, and coating the rolls without an allocation. The single teaspoon of salt spans both cooking waters and a pinch in the dough without an allocation. Water quantities are absent. The page allows fresh cooked-and-cooled potatoes or potatoes cooked and riced the previous day; the graph follows the primary fresh-potato instructions. Suggested mushroom sauces and goulash are accompaniments, not included recipes.

## Trinxat de la Cerdanya

- Editorial descriptor: A Pyrenean cabbage-and-potato cake crushed into garlic-and-rancio-scented bacon fat, crusted like a tortilla, and crowned with crisp bacon.
- Source: [Catalunya.com — Trinxat de la Cerdanya: recepta tradicional i autèntica](https://www.catalunya.com/ca/continguts/article/trinxat-de-la-cerdanya-26-1-97)
- Provenance: Official Catalan tourism portal; complete Catalan-language recipe.
- Exact evidence: 1 winter cabbage or col de pell de galàpet; 600–800 g potatoes; 150–200 g streaky bacon or cured pork belly; 2 garlic cloves; salt and black pepper; 1 glass rancio wine; optional EVOO; optional pork lard or bacon fat; 4 people; 45–55 minutes. Peel and cut potatoes large; wash and separate cabbage leaves, removing a central rib only if very thick; boil together in salted water 25–30 minutes until very tender; brown and reserve crisp bacon while leaving the fat; lightly brown sliced garlic, reduce rancio wine for 1 minute, crush in drained vegetables until homogeneous but textured, flatten and brown for a few minutes per side, then top with bacon, optionally drizzle oil, adjust seasoning, and serve very hot.
- Ambiguities and limitations: The method offers lard or olive oil for frying while rendered bacon fat also remains in the pan. The listed glass of wine becomes only "a splash" in the method, leaving a possible remainder. Olive oil appears both as a frying alternative and an optional finishing drizzle without allocation. Salad, country bread, fried egg, and sautéed black pudding are serving suggestions rather than ingredients in the core recipe.
