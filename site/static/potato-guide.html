The short answer · Denmark

Yukon Gold is a behaviour, not a border.

A yellow-fleshed, middle-of-the-road potato: creamy enough to mash, firm enough to keep some shape. In Denmark, start with Folva — then let the dish overrule you.

Take me to the market ↓
First, ignore the passport

Buy the texture the recipe needs

Variety names change at borders. Cooking behaviour does not. Every bag in the world sits somewhere on this one scale.

A · Firm / waxy

Stays in one piece

Potato salad, boiling whole, soups with distinct pieces, tidy gratin slices, rösti that should bind.

firm · waxy · salad · boiling · kogefast

B · All-purpose

The Yukon Gold zone

Everyday roasting, creamy mash, casseroles, soups, wedges — anything that does not need an extreme.

all-purpose · yellow · type B · slightly floury

C · Floury / starchy

Breaks into fluff

Airy mash, baked potatoes, gnocchi, croquettes, crisp roast edges, thickening a soup by accident.

floury · mealy · starchy · baking · melet

Skin colour is not evidence. Red, yellow, and brown skins each cover the whole scale. So do season, soil, and how long the sack sat in the shop.

DenmarkDanmark
If the recipe says Yukon Gold

Buy Folva first.

The safest Danish one-bag substitute: it is sold for boiling, mash, baking, and wedges all at once. Maya is the other broad option; Melody leans usefully toward mash and baking.

A · keep shape
Sava, Ditta, or Santera; bags marked kogning or kartoffelsalat
B · middle
Folva, Maya — look for several uses printed on the bag
C · more fluffy
Melody, or a bag marked til mos / melede kartofler

Danish reality: use labels and stocked varieties rotate. If the bag lists both boiling and mash, you are in Yukon Gold territory.

The dish has the final vote

Read the next verb before you buy

Salad · soup pieces · neat gratin

Move firmer

Denmark: Sava, Ditta, Santera. Elsewhere: type A / firm / waxy.

Roast · wedges · casserole

Stay in the middle

Denmark: Folva or Maya. Elsewhere: type B / all-purpose.

Creamy mash · chowder

Middle, leaning soft

Folva, Melody, Maya. Go floury only if drier and lighter is welcome.

Gnocchi · croquettes · fluffy mash

Forget “Yukon”, go floury

The technique wants low moisture far more than yellow colour.

Fries · crisp roast potatoes

Middle to floury

More starch, crisper edges. A frying label beats a perfect name match.

Boiled whole · fondant · hasselback

Keep enough structure

Firm, or the firmer half of all-purpose, so the shape survives the tongs.

American recipe decoder

Four names you will keep meeting

Medium starch · yellow flesh · all-purpose

Translate to “balanced”

In Denmark, begin with Folva. Elsewhere, choose type B, all-purpose, slightly floury, or the middle label used locally.

High starch · dry · floury

Translate to “floury”

Buy a baking or mashing potato: type C, mehligkochend, mjölig, jauhoinen, or the local equivalent.

Lower starch · moist · firm

Translate to “firm or waxy”

Buy salad or boiling potatoes: type A, festkochend, fast, or Sava/Ditta in Denmark.

Small, elongated, usually firm

Translate by shape and texture

Look for small waxy salad potatoes, ratte/aspargeskartofler, or another long firm variety. “New potatoes” are a seasonal fallback, not the same cultivar.

When the bag tells you nothing

Cook one before you commit the whole sack

  1. 01Simmer one potato until a knife reaches the centre without argument.
  2. 02Cut it. Clean moist slice = firm. Feathered edges = middle. Cracks and crumbs = floury.
  3. 03Write the variety down. Names drift with season and storage. Your own note still beats the skin colour.
Evidence & limits

A practical translation, not a claim of identical varieties

Yukon Gold, Folva, and every named alternative are distinct cultivars. The recommendations match cooking behaviour and common retail language. Harvest timing, soil, weather, and storage can change the texture of the same variety, so the dish and the bag’s use label outrank the name.
