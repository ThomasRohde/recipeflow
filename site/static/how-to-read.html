The marks that do not change

Learn four things once

The shape moves from view to view, but the underlying recipe does not.

Ingredient or material
Something available to be used: a raw ingredient, a prepared mixture, or a held portion.
Action
A change such as slice, season, bake, or serve. Time, heat, and “until” tests stay with it.
Dependency
An action can only happen after the food or standing condition it needs is ready.
Output
What an action makes. Words such as FINAL, HELD, and WASTE carry meaning without relying on colour.
Map A · route

Flow

Best for tracing an ingredient through time.

Read left → right
  1. Start at an ingredient lane. Each horizontal lane carries a source ingredient or a material made earlier.
  2. Meet the action boxes. A box crosses the lanes it consumes. Read its heat, time, and completion test inside the same box.
  3. Keep following the line. A new label after an action is the material that action produced. A split becomes two paths; a join brings paths together.
  4. Stop at the named output. Final food, garnish, held portions, and waste are written explicitly at the end of their routes.

Use it when you need to answer “where did this ingredient go?” or see parallel prep at a glance.

Map B · span

Compact Table

Best for a dense, ingredient-first overview.

Read rows ↓ then across →
  1. Find source ingredients on the left. Each ingredient owns a row.
  2. Read the height of an action. Its bordered span covers exactly the ingredient rows that feed it, including ingredients folded in through earlier actions.
  3. Move right for later work. Nested actions depend on the material to their left. Independent actions may sit in the same column when their rows do not overlap.
  4. Finish in the output column. The lightly distinguished cells at the far right name what the recipe leaves you with.

Use it when you want the whole recipe in the smallest practical footprint or need to compare ingredient groups.

Map C · account

Kitchen Ledger

Best for checking every transfer and held portion.

Read top ↓ bottom
  1. Check standing conditions first. Oven heat, prepared equipment, and other setup appear in a band above the numbered entries.
  2. Read one entry at a time. The left column says what the action consumes; the centre names the action; the right says what it produces.
  3. Use folio references as breadcrumbs. M1 means material made by entry 1. F1 means a final output. S1 points back to a standing condition.
  4. Trust the printed status words. HELD, FINAL, WASTE, GARNISH, and optional state exactly how an item stands.

Use it when precision matters: scaling, handoff, print, troubleshooting, or checking that no portion quietly disappears.
